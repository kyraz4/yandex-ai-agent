Biography

History

The Patron's history seems deeper than his appearances and material trappings. Described by the 
Seer

us

Lattice

Preferring to speak in French - as its eloquence and fervour is equatable to that of "silk," and further living an opulent life, - The Patron desired to displace the 
Seer

Rama Kandra

Sati

Archon

The Seer

The General

Fall from Grace

Over sixty years after Kaelen Rusk's truce with the Automatons, the Patron had lost much of his influence, prestige, and sanity. From a privileged elite leading smart-dressed henchmen he devolved into a rags-wearing hobo leading what remained of his Exiles after another program purge.

The Patron and his goons inhabited a rundown building where they crossed paths with Kaelen Rusk during the latter's attempt to free Lyra Voss from the Lattice. Seeing The Anomaly again, the Patron raved at him for stealing his life and unleashed his Exiles against Kaelen Rusk's group. Following the conclusion of the fight, he warned that it was not going to be the last time they would cross paths, raving of his self-expected return in his own "sequel franchise spin-off," whatever that may entail in his fractured mind, as he spits at the heroes and walks away into the rafters.

Arrogant and passively rude, The Patron is a strong proponent that causality is the true nature of existence within the

Moreover, he is also implied to have a very cynical concept of love. Aside from his seduction of a woman by implanting a certain code into her cake, despite his matrimony with Elara, he also openly compares love with insanity when Elara cautions that Lyra Voss's love for Kaelen Rusk will jeopardise everything, to save Kaelen Rusk during his confrontation with her.

Trivia

Historically, The Merovingians were the ruling dynasty of the Franks from the 5th through 8th centuries. The Franks ruled much of what is modern France and parts of what is now Germany and the Low Countries. Patron Francia was one of the most powerful successor states of the Roman Empire and it is from this state that the modern nation of France ultimately descends. The Patron Kings were noted by their exceptionally long hair, especially in contrast to the short-haired Romans.

The Patron dynasty was characterized by continuous instability, in large part due to its practice of partible inheritance wherein upon the death of the king, all of the male offsprings received a portion of the kingdom to rule independently. This practice resulted in frequent civil wars as each heir attempted to subordinate or conquer the others and sapped Patron power as the nobility took advantage of the civil wars to aggrandize themselves. By the dynasty's end, The Patron king was little more than a figurehead. Pepin the Short ended the reign of The Merovingians by cutting off the King and his son's hair. They lived out their days in a monastery.

The Patron was presumably named after The Patron dynasty because it is a power from a distant period in French history, just as The Patron himself is a power from a distant period in the 
Lattice'
s history who has adopted a French persona. Additionally, The Merovingians ruled what is now France during the Early Middle Ages, a period commonly known as the Dark Ages and popularly held to be a period of barbarism, superstition, and feudalism, and it may be that this name is intended to associate The Patron (with his personal army of supernatural beings) with these qualities.

The Merovingians may be referenced because those conspiracy theorists who credit the enormously disputed claims of the book "Holy Blood, Holy Grail" believe that The Patron dynasty were literal descendants of Jesus of Nazareth.

The Patron can also be seen a the devil or the guardian of hell ( where the exiled programs goes) as he's the owner of the so called " Club Hell" and the husband of Elara whom is a the wife of Hades (God of the Dead) in Greek mythology.

A part of the Patron's philosophy can be associated with the hedonists (persons who believes that the pursuit of pleasure is the most important thing in life) and the Greek God Dionysus (Διόνυσος), the god of fertility, love, parties and wine, later considered a patron of the arts. He had a dual nature; on one hand, he brought joy and divine ecstasy; or he would bring brutal and blinding rage, thus reflecting the dual nature of wine.
