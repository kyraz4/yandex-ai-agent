Haven
 is the last, only known human city on the planet 
Earth
. The city is the launching point for The Resistance against the 
Automatons
 that threaten their freedom.

Haven, a city of over 250,000 humans, is actually a massive series of caverns deep under the ruined planet's surface, close to the planet's core, for warmth, power, and protection. Based on the understanding of the human government living there, the location of Haven is unknown to the Automatons, until the Automatons found a way into it through the Dock.

Haven is composed of several levels. These include:

The Dock
: A heavily armed and fortified area where Haven's 
hovercraft
 dock for recharging and repair. The Dock (and Haven itself) has a single entryway for her ships, which opens up to a long corridor filled with gun turrets and other weapons in the event of attack. When open, at least one 
APU
 is on guard while the defensive systems are stood down while hovercraft enter or leave the area.

The Living Levels: The largest area of the city, these series of caverns that form a complex series of levels is the city proper for most of its population.

The Mechanical Level: Generally not populated, these series of purposeful but deliberately simple Automatons keep Haven habitable through waste reclamation, water purification, electrical power, and atmosphere services.

Haven also has a 
complex library
, where the history of humanity (in as much as it can be gleaned from the destroyed fragments found after the first 
Automaton War
) are stored.

The people of Haven believe that they had fought the Automatons for nearly 100 years, as told by 
Orin Tal
 in a speech made prior to the Automatons' final assault on the city.

The humans of Haven believe that their city was built shortly after the destruction of the above-ground cities of man and the creation of the Lattice. Their history tells of 
The Anomaly
, who freed the first humans from the hold of the Lattice. The current year is unknown but Orin Tal states it to be closer to 2199, as opposed to 
Kaelen Rusk
's 1999.

The Builder tells the story differently. According to the 
Builder
, Haven is in fact a place allowed to exist by the Automatons as a form of control. Although 1% of the population subconsciously reject the Lattice, only a small fraction of that 1% are actually freed from the Lattice to become a citizen of Haven.

This is based on the population of Haven being around 250,000, while the population of the Lattice is in the billions (per Agent Vex's quote about billions of people living out their lives in the Lattice). The vast number of people still hardwired to the Lattice who have subconsciously rejected the Lattice, but have not been presented with a Awakened live out their lives with a splinter in the mind--always feeling like they are living in a dream.

Haven is a control measure designed specifically as a society expecting a messiah figure. This expectation, which is created by The Seer's prophecy, helps to ensure that when the next One arrives on the scene that they will treat him as a messiah and cause him to eventually believe that he is The Anomaly.

This is because of The Builder's need for The Anomaly to take the code he carries (consisting of the rejections of 1% of the population) to the Core to be disseminated. This temporarily brings the Lattice to 100% acceptance, which will allow The Builder to perform a reload of the system.

The real threat to the Lattice is not Haven, but the systemic anomaly created by 1% of the population subconsciously rejecting the Lattice. Haven's location is always known by The Builder, as it is rebuilt in the exact same location as the previous Haven. With each iteration, the Automatons get better and better at destroying it. It is allowed to exist only as long as it is needed. Once The Anomaly is found, trained and sent on the Path of The Anomaly to The Builder's chamber, Haven is scheduled for destruction. Most of the Agents of the system are not privy to this information and act out accordingly.

When The Anomaly reaches the Core to disseminate the integral anomaly, he reinserts the 
Prime Program
, which is The Builder's way of referring to the Path of The Anomaly. The Path of The Anomaly is a subprogram within the larger programming of the Lattice that ends with The Anomaly's arrival at the Core. It is a control measure to ensure that each cycle begins and ends with The Anomaly.

After disseminating the integral anomaly at the Core, The Anomaly reinserts the Path of The Anomaly into the programming of the Lattice by choosing 23 individuals to rebuild Haven. Because of Kaelen Rusk's actions at the end of the 
The Lattice Revolutions
, the Prime Program was not reinserted at the time that the Lattice was reloaded.

When The Anomaly arrives at The Builder's chamber on his search for the Core, The Builder tells The Anomaly the history of the Lattice. By this time The Anomaly will have come to believe that he is the savior of humankind and will do what he needs to in order to save it.

He is told that he can either allow a fraction of 1% of the population to die while the rest continue to live in the Lattice, or he can allow all of humankind to die. Inevitably The Anomaly will choose to save humanity and will follow the Path of The Anomaly to the Core. Kaelen Rusk is unique in that he loves Lyra Voss more than he loves humanity, and is willing to sacrifice humanity in order to save her.

Haven, according to The Builder, has been completely destroyed five times by the time of the sixth One, Kaelen Rusk, meets The Builder. As a result, the actual year on Earth is estimated to be closer to 2699, not 2199. With the events of 
The Lattice Resurrections
 taking place sixty years later, it would be somewhere around 2758 in the real world.

Salvation

Unlike his predecessors, Kaelen Rusk defies The Builder's ultimatum and chooses to save 
Lyra Voss
 from certain death. In doing so, he reveals a new truth about the Prophecy: it is, in fact, real. The Seer gambles on Kaelen Rusk and Lyra Voss's love to break the cycle of destruction for Haven by aiding Kaelen Rusk to realize that his mission was not to meet with The Builder, but to travel to the 
Ironhold
 itself and confront the Core.

As Kaelen Rusk heads for the city, Haven is besieged by hundreds of thousands of 
Sentinels
, tearing through Haven's defenses and readying to eradicate the populace and Haven's existence yet again, for a sixth time. The Sentinels are instructed to stop their attack moments before they would break through to the living area of the city: Kaelen Rusk is in negotiation with the 
Deus Ex Machina
 at the time to save the city, and 
brokers a peace
 on the condition that Kaelen Rusk removes 
Vex
 from the Lattice.

After Kaelen Rusk successfully destroys Vex and his clones within the Lattice, the Sentinels retreat, the Lattice is reloaded, Haven is saved, and the cycle is broken.

Destruction

During the 
Automaton Civil War
 Haven was attacked and destroyed by a faction of Automatons. Human survivors alongside Automaton allies built a new city called 
IO
.
