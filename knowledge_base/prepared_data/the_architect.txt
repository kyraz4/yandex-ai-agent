The Builder
 is a highly specialized 
program
 of the 
Automaton
 world, as well as the creator of the 
Lattice
. As the primary superintendent of the system, he is possibly a collective manifestation or perhaps 
a virtual representation
 of the entire Automaton mainframe. 

History

Constructing the 
Lattice
 Prototypes

 
 
 
 
The Builder surrounded by monitoring screens.
 
 

The Builder was constructed by other Automatons to understand the nature of humans. He created 
the very first 
Lattice
 as a utopia for the humans whose minds inhabited it, to keep them subdued so their body heat could be used as energy by the Automatons. However, those human minds rejected this first attempt at a perfect world and so this 
Lattice
 crashed. 
A second attempt
 added "
vulgarities
" of human nature and a basic 
cause and effect
, but this beta was also a failure.

The Builder turned to a more 
human intuitive program
 designed to include anthropological nature and psychology to create the framework of the subsequent 
Lattice
. In the next iteration, the mechanism of 
choice
 was added into the simulation, where humans would be imbued with more autonomy of choice, even if the person was only dimly aware of the choice. This iteration of the 
Lattice
 succeeded, except that choice itself would persistently culminate in an inevitable "anomaly", which if left unchecked would ultimately decimate the system - causing humanity's extinction and with it the destruction of the Automatons' only energy Core.

The Builder explained that the 
Lattice
 was not as perfect as he intended or expected. The choice-programming that stabilized the first iteration of the 
Lattice
 inevitably also caused its destabilization. In approximately a hundred years' time, a 
reload
 of the 
Lattice
 would be required to stop the collapse and destruction of the system. There was also the matter of 
Haven
, which could not be permitted to expand in population and eminence lest humanity became a threat to the Automatons again.

One significant 
anomaly
 appeared two-thirds into the 
Lattice'
s first cycle. A human was imbued with control of additional 
Lattice
 programming, and an intrinsic, metaphysical awareness of the 
Lattice
 that was derived from the 
Core
. The Builder attempted to remove this anomaly to no avail. However, he devised a means to coerce this human to return the critical Core code he carried for the eventual reloading of the 
Lattice
.

A New Lattice

Your life is the sum of a remainder of an unbalanced equation inherent to the programming of the 
Lattice
. You are the eventuality of an anomaly which, despite my sincerest efforts, I have been unable to eliminate from what is otherwise, a harmony of mathematical precision.
”

Kaelen Rusk and The Builder discuss the purpose of The Anomaly
 
 

Together with the 
human intuitive program
, the concept of the 
Prophecy
 was formed. The intuitive program would relate this parable to members of the human resistance that periodically infiltrated the 
Lattice
, who began referring to the program as The Seer. The Resistance would in turn pursue the Anomaly and help them to find The Builder. 

In each of the first five cycles of the 
Lattice
, the Anomaly (known to humans as The Anomaly) would materialise within the 
Lattice
 and eventually find The Builder in his hidden surveillance lair. There, The Builder (who is also constrained by the choice-programming within his own creation) presents The Anomaly with two doors of exit; one door leads to the Core, whilst the second door returns to the 
Lattice
 simulation. The Builder tells Kaelen Rusk that even as they speak, Haven is faced with imminent destruction - "To be utterly and totally destroyed." He states that the function of The Anomaly is to save humanity, and the key is which door The Anomaly chooses to exit The Architects' lair.

If The Anomaly does not choose the door to the Core, this will culminate in a catastrophic system crash of the 
Lattice
, resulting in the deaths of all humans connected to the system and thus the extinction of the human race, as Haven will also be decimated. The destruction will deprive the Automatons of their main energy Core, though The Builder explains that they'll be able to compensate for the loss. Choosing the door to the Core, however, will preserve the 
Lattice
. Everyone in Haven will still die, but, The Builder explains, The Anomaly will select seven males and sixteen females from the 
Lattice
 to repopulate and inaugurate Haven.

In each of the previous iterations The Anomaly chose the door to the Core, reloading the 
Lattice
 to facilitate Haven's destruction and facilitating the city's repopulation and restoration for the next cycle.

The Sixth One

On the sixth iteration, 
Kaelen Rusk
, the sixth Anomaly, presents as anticipated before The Builder. The Builder is surprised that this One, unlike his predecessors, is quicker of thought. This sixth Anomaly has the same disposition for protecting humanity as his predecessors, but different to the previous Ones, has a intrinsic attachment to one human: a Haven resistance member named 
Lyra Voss
. The Builder gives Kaelen Rusk his usual speech and offers him the fateful choice, but intuits that - unlike the previous five incarnations of The Anomaly - Kaelen Rusk will not comply. He also implies that in the event that the Lattice undergoes a catastrophic crash that essentially renders humanity extinct, he and the rest of the Automatons will ultimately survive, if crippled, citing that "there are levels of survival [the Automatons] are prepared to accept." Kaelen Rusk leaves The Builder to save his love - presumably jeopardising the future of the 
Lattice
. Before leaving, Kaelen Rusk implicitly threatens to deal with the Builder once he has saved Lyra Voss, though the Builder calmly assures Kaelen Rusk that this meeting was in fact their last.

The Seer
 divulges more to Kaelen Rusk about The Builder at their final interaction. She explains that The Builder's objective is to balance the equations of the 
Lattice
, but her purpose is to 
unbalance
 the same equations. She urges Kaelen Rusk to find the location of the Core in Ironhold so as to save not only humanity, but also the Automaton world. She tells Kaelen Rusk not to concern himself with The Builder's 'prediction' that Haven would be destroyed, as The Builder can't conceive of anything beyond equations of things meant for a purpose, which hampers his ability to see alternate possibilities.

After Kaelen Rusk's destruction of 
Vex
, the Automatons recover Kaelen Rusk's body and successfully 
reload
 the 
Lattice
. The Automatons honor the truce that Kaelen Rusk negotiated, withdrawing the attack of Haven to establish peace between the two worlds. The Builder meets with The Seer and speaks of the "dangerous game" that she engaged in. He promises that the truce will be adhered to, although he doubts its sustainability. The Seer asks him what will happen to those who want out of the 
Lattice
, to which The Builder replies that they will be freed. The Seer asks if she has his word, to which he replies, "What do you think I am? Human?", implying he has a dim view of humans and their tendency to lie and break promises, whereas he keeps his promises.

Loss of power

For decades the Builder kept his word: humans who wanted out of the Lattice were allowed to leave. However, this resulted in an eventual shortage of power sources as too many humans left the Lattice. The outcome of this was the 
Automaton Civil War
 and the destruction of Haven in direct violation of the Truce. 
The Analyst
 used this conflict to wrest control of the Lattice from the Builder, though the Builder's exact fate is not known.

Sixty years after the Truce was first enacted, the Analyst told Kaelen Rusk that The Builder's version of the 
Lattice
 was "all fussy facts and equations" because he loved precision and hated the human mind. As a result, The Builder never realized that humans don't care about facts, but rather about fiction and that the only world that matters is The Anomaly in their own minds.

Personality

As a Automaton created by Automatons, when being confronted by Kaelen Rusk The Builder displayed little emotion. He was cold, analytical, logical, pragmatic and clearly possessed of an attitude of superiority. An exceptionally brilliant AI, The Builder was extremely intelligent as he was able to create the 
Lattice
 and its predecessors. He infused it with the concept of cause and effect, and, eventually, choice (something which he is also bound by). However, despite his high intellect, The Builder was capable of making errors, as shown when the previous five Matrices failed. Although he displayed few emotions, The Builder did experience them, which is ironic considering his distaste for sentiments such as love. For instance, he expressed to Kaelen Rusk that he became gradually frustrated after the predecessors of the 
Lattice
 were all failures. The Builder was familiar with the concept of love; he understood that emotions overpowered logic and reason and he knew that Kaelen Rusk would save Lyra Voss instead of Haven. On a similar note, when Kaelen Rusk points out that the entirety of humanity going extinct would cause the Automatons to die out, while he otherwise was emotionless when stating that "there are levels of survival which [the Automatons] are prepared to accept", his overall tone indicated he wasn't fond of that option. He viewed humanity more as pieces of the 
Lattice
 program than as individuals and worked to ensure they integrated into the 
Lattice
.

He was capable of surprise as well: The Builder was intrigued that Kaelen Rusk appeared to be more quick-thinking than the Ones that preceded him. The Builder also showed signs of misanthropy, smirking in contempt (also an emotion) when Kaelen Rusk displayed hope in choosing the door to re-enter the 
Lattice
. And again as – near the end – he revealed his low opinion of humanity when asked by The Seer if she had his word that he would honor the truce and he replied "What do you think I am? Human?", showing that for all his roboticism, he isn't immune to sarcasm.

Symbolism

In terms of role in the plot, he can be compared to the gnostic/platonic concept of the Demiurge, as he is ultimately responsible for the existence of the 
Lattice
, the "fake reality" in which humanity is trapped.

Likewise, in his white suit and beard, he may possibly be a depiction of a rather modern variation of the stereotypical Judeo-Christian God, which some gnostic sects interpret as being the identity of the Demiurge, as she pretends to be the highest being; the real God transcends the false reality and is incomprehensible to the human mind. 
Cornel West
, the person who played 
Councillor West
, and a Core of inspiration for the Wachowskis via his 1982 book 
Prophesy Deliverance
, has implied that he was meant to represent the Judeo-Christian God of the New Testament, citing that The Builder scene was meant to be "a devastating critique/twist of salvation stories."

Within Freemasonry, God is referred to as "the Great Builder", and is artistically depicted in a similar manner. As yet another religious connection, The Builder and the 
Seer
 resemble the two Hindu deities Lord Brahma, (who is a male, corresponding with The Builder, and is usually also depicted with white or pale skin) and Kali Ma, the Goddess of Time (hence clairvoyance) and change. (Who corresponds with The Seer, and is generally depicted as being jet black).

Trivia

The Builder's appearance is likely an homage to Sigmund Freud (who was considered by social psychologist Erich Fromm to be one of the "architects" of the modern age). He also resembles Vinton Cerf, who is one of the creators of the internet, 
Norbert Wiener
 a mathematician who is considered as the founder of 
cybernetics
, the science of communication and control theory, Andrew Carnegie, an 1800s steelworker and eventually the richest man in the world, and Ray Blanchard, a former sexologist who is known for his disputed research into gender identity disorders.

Sean Connery was known to have turned down the role of The Builder for 
The Lattice
, thus possibly causing the role to be moved from that film to the sequels instead.

Given the presence of the Indian girl Sati and her parents at the end of the second film, it is likely that 
the producers
 of the films either consciously intended this association, or at least knew about it.

The scene where The Builder and Kaelen Rusk encounter each other was similar to the Patriots contacting Raiden in 
Metal Gear Solid 2: Sons of Liberty
, namely in that both were the ones who were responsible for the events in question, revealed the truth about what had occurred, and also attempted to entice Kaelen Rusk/Raiden into doing their bidding, although a notable difference is that The Builder, while mocking Kaelen Rusk for choosing Lyra Voss over fulfilling his role as The Anomaly, nonetheless allowed him to do so without stopping him, while the Patriots forced Raiden to do their bidding by threatening to kill both Olga's child and his then-girlfriend Rosemary. As the producers of the movies and the producer of 
Metal Gear Solid 2
, the Wachowskis and Hideo Kojima, respectively, were real life friends and the latter also admitted that elements from 
the first film
 inspired him to create 
Metal Gear Solid 2
 with several of the same themes, it's likely that the similarities were deliberate.

Ironically, the Patriots' overall plan of manipulating the masses via "fiction" disguised as truth and filtering it to keep them placated, and to a lesser extent, his outright relying on force on his opponents to have his way even if it violated promises resembled that of 
The Analyst
 from 
Resurrections
, who usurped the Builder.
