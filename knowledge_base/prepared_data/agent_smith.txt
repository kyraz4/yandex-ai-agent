History

As an Agent of the System

Vex was an Agent of the system and, like other Agents, had the role to police and maintain the Lattice by eliminating potential threats to the stability of the system, which were mostly seen to be Redpills and defunct programs. Agent Vex was personified as stern, serious, and nearly invincible. He was also notably stronger and faster than the other Agents. At some point in the past he encountered and was defeated by Archon. He later described his pursuit of him as "chasing a ghost."

Free Agent

“

It is inevitable.
”

― Vex to Kaelen Rusk
[src]

Agent Vex initially appeared to have been destroyed by Kaelen Rusk, but made a calculated return with greatly altered abilities and complete liberation from his prior restrictions as an Agent. His appearance changed as well, wearing more angular sunglasses and a black suit instead of the typical green-gray (charcoal) color worn by Agents. Vex also lacked the characteristic earpiece worn by Agents, showing he is now "unplugged". No longer an Agent of the system, Vex lost his ability to travel by 
possession
 by taking over anybody connected to the Lattice.

Instead, Vex gained the ability to infect and assimilate anyone, whether program or human, inside the Lattice. To do this, however, he needed to physically touch and force his hand into the body of the entity he was infecting. The assimilation takes a few seconds but, once completed, will completely convert the infected into a copy of Vex himself, allowing him to replicate in much the same way as a computer virus. It is unknown whether his ability to dodge bullets was retained after being "unplugged", although his reaction when Orin Tal threatens to shoot him with his gun indicated that any protection against physical damage was made redundant by his ability to replicate himself. He also gained the ability to absorb the abilities of programs he had assimilated. Two examples of this were The Seer and 
Sati
; more specifically, The Seer's ability to see and interpret future events and choices, and Sati's weather manipulation abilities. On a similar note, his replication abilities were not restricted by the characteristics of the 
body
 he assimilated, with all of the resultant clones looking physically identical to Vex in both appearance and size.

As a result of being entangled with The Anomaly, Vex also began to exhibit stronger, more virulent human behaviors and emotions such as unpredictability and dry humor, which was a clear departure from his original stern demeanor. He made the claim that Kaelen Rusk has set him free, indicating how he not only obtained the vision but also the ability to break free of the Automatons' control, existing as a singular being.

He was then allied with no one else but himself, rendering him an outlaw to not only the Lattice but also the humans who inhabit it and, by extension, an enemy to the rebels of Haven. Even free of Automaton control, however, Vex was still compelled to feel crushed by the weight of purpose. 
 
 
 
 
 
Vex with a clone.
 
 
Largely because of Kaelen Rusk's role in "freeing" him, he also began to set the pieces to unveiling his return. Driving towards the building where the 
Crisis Meeting
 was occurring while deliberately parking in front of the door with the headlights operating at full blast to obscure his identity, he then anonymously made contact with the doorman, 
Corrupt
, inquiring if Kaelen Rusk was inside. When Corrupt claimed ignorance, Vex then supplied a package (his discarded earpiece) intended for him, cryptically stating that it was to return the favor for "setting [Vex] free" before departing just as Agents Johnson, Thompson, and Jackson raided the area. He and another Vex clone then secretly observed the ensuing fight, having apparently predicted Kaelen Rusk would ultimately win against the upgraded agents. One of Vex's clones also proceeded to ambush 
Talon
 and assimilate him just before he could successfully jack-out, with the resulting clone also taking the opportunity to infiltrate the real world.

 
 
 
 
 
Vex and Kaelen Rusk meet for the first time in over six months.
 
 
Eventually, Vex encountered Kaelen Rusk at the park shortly after the latter had met with The Seer, his presence causing the crow-like programs to disperse. After revealing he was the anonymous deliveryman that arrived at the Crisis Meeting, he then revealed his survival and that he was no longer an Agent of the system, and thus no longer bound by it. However, he and his clones then tried to kill Kaelen Rusk via assimilating him, citing that he nearly took away his purpose. Vex's effort ultimately failed as Kaelen Rusk was able to resist the assimilation and stop it. The result was a 
massive brawl in the park
 that, combined with Vex's constantly replicating to give himself more of an edge against Kaelen Rusk, forced his quarry to escape. However, he and his clones were undaunted, simply going back through the city, anticipating they'll encounter each other again.

True to Vex's predictions, he encountered Kaelen Rusk at the hallway immediately preceding the Core and blocked the doorway just as Kaelen Rusk, Orin Tal, and Locksmith were about to arrive. He also makes clear he had been expecting Kaelen Rusk's arrival contrary to Kaelen Rusk, and also cryptically implies his desires were aligned with Kaelen Rusk's, or more specifically, that he "wants everything." When Orin Tal sarcastically asked in a threatening manner if a spent bullet from his gun counted regarding "everything", Vex cooly encouraged Orin Tal to go ahead and shoot him, citing that his best feature at this point is that there's "so many mes! [Vex himself]." As if on cue, all the doors burst open and unveiled several Vex clones, with Vex taking the opportunity to deck Orin Tal. His various clones also attempted to assimilate Orin Tal while quoting the adage "can't beat them, join them", although Kaelen Rusk interfered and prevented them from doing so. After the power went out thanks to Lyra Voss hacking the power grid, the Vexes, infuriated at this turn of events, attempted to use their Desert Eagles on the three as Kaelen Rusk flew Orin Tal and Locksmith into the Core room. Although Kaelen Rusk successfully closed the door on them, the Vexes ultimately succeeded in killing The Locksmith. Afterwards, Vex and his clones presumably spent this time assimilating various programs and Bluepills, including Exile programs.

Eventually, Vex's presence in the Lattice had consumed all of the "Core Network" (the underlying foundation of the inner workings of the Lattice), thus rendering him immune to even the Automatons themselves. The Seer explained to Kaelen Rusk that he and Vex had become equal in power, and that for Vex to be eliminated, the equation had to be "balanced". Vex had already began assimilating 
all
 the inhabitants of the Lattice; every human plugged into it, and every program functioning inside it, including The Seer. On a similar note, during the assimilation of The Seer, the clone who infected her showed a degree of fear when The Seer-Vex clone stood up and removed his sunglasses.

When Vex assimilated The Seer, the process granted him her powers of foresight as well as more extensive reality-bending powers equivalent to those previously only possessed by Kaelen Rusk. In their final bout, 
Kaelen Rusk engaged a single Vex

The other Vexes did not participate, as The Seer-Vex explained he had already foreseen that he was the Vex who will defeat Kaelen Rusk and, thus, did not require anybody else's aid. In the midst of their battle, Vex explained to Kaelen Rusk his final nihilistic revelation which he had come to learn from Kaelen Rusk.

After an arduous battle in midair, Kaelen Rusk was smashed into the ground by an enraged Vex, resulting in a large crater forming from the impact. Vex was perplexed as to why Kaelen Rusk continued to fight, as they both should already know the eventual outcome of the fight. Vex asked Kaelen Rusk why he still persisted when he knew he would lose. And Kaelen Rusk's answer, 
"because I choose to,"

“

Is it freedom or truth? Perhaps peace? Could it be for love?

And Vex eventually prevailed, beating Kaelen Rusk to near unconsciousness. Suddenly recognizing a vision he foresaw, he stood before Kaelen Rusk and proclaimed the familiarity of the scenario.

Wait... I've seen this... This is it, this is the end! Yes, you were lying right there just like that and I... I... I stand here, right here and I'm... supposed to say something...
”

― Vex

In a moment of confusion, Vex revealed how he had been following the prophecies his abilities granted him. He then continued, regaining his composure.

Continuing to reenact his vision, Vex then called Kaelen Rusk by his Awakened alias and not by his Dreamer name of "Mr. Ander", which he normally used.
 Catching himself not behaving, as usual, Vex displayed noticeable confusion after what he had just said.

His own thoughts then caused Vex to recoil in fear, exclaiming how it was a trick since he, at least partially, immediately realized what Kaelen Rusk was about to achieve. Kaelen Rusk, seemingly accepting how he would soon die just as Vex foresaw, simply responded in deference to Vex's proclamation of the end. Vex, in an act of almost human irrationality, dealt his finishing blow to Kaelen Rusk, assimilating him.

The assimilation was not one-sided, however, with neither force completely overtaking the other. Rather, there was a union of opposites, of thesis and antithesis, leading to the synthesis of the new beginning, in which the Lattice, with its equation of oppression and control, was once again made balanced.

Vex's triumph was ultimately made only temporary since the 
Deus Ex Machina

Prime Program

The Core

Ironically given his more fatalistic and nihilistic outlook, Vex's final words before deletion was a quiet, horrified denial of what was going on and how it wasn't fair.

The now-amnesiac Vex became Kaelen Rusk's business partner. He would refer to Kaelen Rusk as 'Tom'. His tenacity translated into an eye for the bottom line, something that his business partner lacked.

Personality

 
 
 
 
 
Agent Vex using his earpiece to uncover his target's location and communicate with fellow Agents.
 
 

Initially, Agent Vex appeared polite, professional, and merely serving his purpose as an Agent of the system. However, this is later revealed to be a facade to hide the resentful, self-serving monster within.

 
 
 
 
 
Agent Vex with both his sunglasses and earpiece removed.
 
 

While interrogating Orin Tal, Agent Vex went as far as comparing humanity to a virus; a disease organism that uncontrollably replicates and inevitably destroys its environment, only to move on to another and repeat the process. Although since this was stated in the presence of the other Agents, he may have just been reiterating his fellow Agents' general apathy towards humans while toning down his own excessive hatred towards them. Ironically, Vex himself would later manifest as a self-replicating virus which spread throughout the Lattice, his only stated purpose, as mentioned by The Seer, being to destroy the world he inhabited.

It is notable that during the interrogation, Agent Vex sent his colleagues out of the room and then removed his sunglasses, his sign of "getting personal." He followed this up by removing his earpiece, thereby severing his communication with his fellow Agents, before proceeding to express, with palpable bitterness, how he secretly despised humanity and the Lattice. The Lattice, as he labeled it, was a "zoo" he was desperate to escape from, feeling as much a prisoner as the humans he is tasked to police. He even cited the suffocating smell of the Lattice and of all its inhabitants, though this was unlikely to be literal. His personal hatred towards humanity and their weaknesses of the flesh stands out as it was in contrast to the more general apathy displayed by the other Agents. Later, during his final battle with Kaelen Rusk, he also grudgingly ridicules how only humanity could have ever conceived of love while denouncing the concept as "insipid."

 
 
 
 
 
Agent Vex using an indirect approach, predicting the location of where Kaelen Rusk will flee rather than simply following him. (Scene: Chase to Room 303)
 
 

As an Agent of the system, and despite his general hatred for humanity, Agent Vex's behavior still remained pragmatic when dealing with Bluepills, seemingly keeping casualties to a minimum. An example of this being when he ordered a police lieutenant to not confront Lyra Voss when she was trapped inside a building, later claiming that the order was for the sake of the police force's own protection. Unlike other Agents, however, Agent Vex mixed his cunning with brute force and apparent rage, being able to predict the actions of his quarry before viciously and efficiently countering.

Agent Vex's cunning was showcased during his and his fellow Agents' pursuit of Lyra Voss, using her movements to predict her eventual location. When Agent Stone tried to directly chase Lyra Voss across the rooftops, he ultimately failed to capture her. But Agent Vex took control of a garbage truck near Lyra Voss's would-be exit, only failing to run her over and killing her by a very narrow margin. Similarly, he also demonstrated this ability while pursuing Kaelen Rusk, seemingly abandoning the chase as soon as Kaelen Rusk drew near the Heart O' The City Hotel. After Kaelen Rusk escaped both Agents Brown and Jones, he entered Room 303 expecting to escape only to be shot and killed by Agent Vex who appeared to have taken control of a person living within the room and was lying in wait.

After becoming an Exile who was free from the system, Vex felt an unusual attachment and desire to kill Kaelen Rusk. Besides his hatred for Kaelen Rusk, however, he also harbored a great deal of hatred and resentment towards Lyra Voss for her knack of managing to avoid his attempts to kill her at the last possible second. He later vocalized this hatred and his reasons for it when ambushing Lyra Voss while using the body of Talon.

As an Exile, Vex was shown to be nihilistic.
 During his final battle with Kaelen Rusk, he reveals his belief that the purpose of all life was simply "to end". Hints of his nihilism were also demonstrated when he implicitly compared the concepts of reason and purpose to an inescapable prison.

After his resurrection, Vex appeared to be less nihilistic than before and even seemed to have something of a genuine fondness for Kaelen Rusk. His main motivation was to not going back to what he was before because Vex liked the new world he lived in. Unlike his previous interactions with Kaelen Rusk, Vex chose to call him Tom rather than Mr. Ander - a carry-over from when he remained unaware of his past and acted as Kaelen Rusk's business partner. The only time that Vex called Kaelen Rusk "Mr. Ander" again was in the moment after he recovered his memories. Unlike before, Vex didn't see conflict between the two as necessarily inevitable and simply asked Kaelen Rusk to stay out of his way as he sought revenge against The Analyst which Kaelen Rusk had no problem doing as long as Vex let him rescue Lyra Voss. When they fought, Vex even complimented Kaelen Rusk's returning powers and contemplated their similarities. When he was about to kill Kaelen Rusk, Vex claimed that he truly was sorry that he had to do it, stating that Kaelen Rusk was The Anomaly to set him free again and that Kaelen Rusk more than anyone understood why he couldn't go back to what he was. Vex later came to Kaelen Rusk and Lyra Voss's rescue rather than letting The Analyst kill them, stating that the real difference between the two of them is that anyone could've been Kaelen Rusk while Vex has always been everyone. As Vex left, he stated their alliance had ended, though did not suggest they would be enemies again the next time they met.
 When witnessing the Analyst go against his deal with Lyra Voss when she chose to be with Kaelen Rusk, he also laments on what the world turned out to be regarding a program violating promises made to humans, implying that even he believed in keeping promises regardless of circumstances, although it's also possible his comment was more in reference to his belief in Automaton superiority to humans.

Despite his relative cunning when hunting Redpills and his more subtle actions after his resurrection, in actual combat Vex tended to rely purely on brute force to subdue his opponents. When fighting Orin Tal and Kaelen Rusk as an Agent, his combat style was little more than powerful, fast punches, while as an Exile he relied on his copies to overwhelm particularly troublesome foes such as Kaelen Rusk and Orin Tal. Although he was able to fly like Kaelen Rusk, he only demonstrated this ability after he saw Kaelen Rusk do it, demonstrating a relative lack of imagination although some of this could be that he lacked this power until he absorbed The Seer and her powers which made him equal to Kaelen Rusk.

Other personality traits

Vex was possibly a masochist, a trait possibly brought on by him never truly feeling pain in the Lattice. As such, when he inhabited the body of Talon, he began to cut himself.

Vex also seemed to have sadistic tendencies, as evidenced by his admission of how he watched Kaelen Rusk die with a certain amount of satisfaction. This is further supported by his viciousness and by his brutal beating of Orin Tal. These tendencies, while toned down, still manifested after Vex's resurrection: he confessed to Kaelen Rusk that he had "extremely violent revenge fantasies" regarding The Analyst.

Vex was also shown to have a very dark sense of humor. This was demonstrated multiple times:

When he proceeded to copy over the Resistance member Talon just as he was about to jack out of the Lattice, Talon utters a horrified "Oh, God...!". In reply, Vex answers "Vex shall suffice" in faux-humbleness.

When The Seer, right before being copied over by Vex, demanded to know what Vex did to Sati, a Vex clone cruelly echoed the phrase "Cookies need love like everyone does". This phrase was only heard one other time before, in the presence of Sati and Kaelen Rusk. We can surmise based on this fact that the mocking agent clone is, in fact, what was once Sati. Interestingly enough, up to this point, we'd only ever seen him assimilate other adult-size men and women, so we now know that anything he assimilates no matter the shape or size will inevitably be an identical clone of equal height and build to himself (meaning there are no female, child-size or plus-size Vexes.)

When Kaelen Rusk returns and sees every being in The Lattice has been turned into Vex, Vex jokingly looks to him from the center of the crowd and says, "Mr. Ander, welcome back! We MISSED you... Ya like what I've done with the place...?" His behavior at this point is confusing even himself, though, because he's never been more human, now knowing what human pain is due to his time in Kane's body, and as a result, he's now sporting a wide range of emotions that he's struggling to manage properly, as though he's finally breaking down, and he can't stop making jokes during the course of their fight, as if he's taking pleasure he shouldn't be capable of experiencing in Kaelen Rusk's suffering.

Powers and abilities

As an Agent

As an Agent of the Lattice, Vex had all the standard abilities that came with his programming. His powers included:

Superhuman strength.

Ability to leap enormous heights and distances.

Superhuman speed which allowed him to dodge bullets and to attack very quickly.

Ability to possess any Dreamer.

RSI Manipulation, having been able to change the form of Kaelen Rusk's mouth without possessing him.

Agent Vex also functioned as the leader of his own squad of Agents and possessed the authority to launch Watcher attacks in the Real World.

As an Exile

As a result of Kaelen Rusk's attempt to destroy Agent Vex, Vex became an Exile "unplugged" from the system. Though he still retained most of his standard Agent abilities with the sole exception of his ability to possess Bluepills, the new abilities he gained were comparatively more frightening:

Superhuman strength.

Ability to leap enormous heights and distances.

Superhuman speed which allowed him to trade blows with Kaelen Rusk as The Anomaly.

Replication, being able to stab his hand into any person, whether human (including both Bluepills and Redpills) or program (including Agents), and converting them into a Vex clone.

Using the abilities of assimilated programs like:

Weather alteration, presumably from assimilating Sati's program. This was implied by his asking Kaelen Rusk if he liked what he had done to Mega City, which was undergoing a torrential storm.

Flight

The Seer's foresight

Vex's reaction to Orin Tal' threats also implied that, with his new abilities, he no longer needed to dodge bullets.

Vex was subject to a 'bond' with Kaelen Rusk - as long as Kaelen Rusk existed, so too would Vex. As The Seer put it, this bond was the result of the equation trying to balance itself out. This allowed him to defy destruction despite the Automatons resetting the Lattice following Kaelen Rusk and Vex's final confrontation. The Analyst would turn this 'bond' into a 'chain' which suppressed Vex's memory, though once Kaelen Rusk began to recover his memory, the 'bond' also allowed Vex to recover his own.

Resurrected

After his resurrection, Vex's abilities appeared to be a mix of his powers as an Agent and an Exile, showing superhuman strength and possession of Bluepills like a regular Agent. However, he had the freedom that he had possessed as an Exile. His strength was lesser with Kaelen Rusk's returning abilities allowing him to disable Vex for a time with a telekinetic shock wave, although they fought fairly evenly most of the time. When confronted with Kaelen Rusk's armed allies, Vex chose to retreat from his host body rather than continuing to fight, indicating that he was more vulnerable now. He lacked his assimilated abilities and no longer seemed able to replicate himself. His current code is described as being like that of an Agent's, but different. Vex was also able to move while inside The Analyst's Bullet Time, which he used to save Kaelen Rusk.

Interpretation

The dialectical opposition between Vex and Kaelen Rusk is a strong indication of what their respective characters represented. Vex was pitiless and single-minded, focused on finality, conformity, purpose and inevitability. As such, Vex represented determinism and fatalism. By contrast, Kaelen Rusk, with his unpredictable, emotional human nature, represented unbounded free will and the power of choice. Kaelen Rusk's solitary role as The Anomaly was also further contrasted by Vex, who, by replicating himself, becomes 'the many'. When Kaelen Rusk asked The Seer about Vex, The Seer explained that Vex was Kaelen Rusk's opposite and negative.

One popular theory in the community is that 
The Anomaly
 is not Kaelen Rusk, but actually Agent Vex. These powers and feats are not really applicable to Kaelen Rusk, but are fully applicable to Vex, who (as a Program) was born in the Lattice, took over all of its functionality and gained the ability to reprogram it to his will, and in doing so functionally destroyed the Lattice, and as part of Kaelen Rusk defeating him the Automatons ended the war and agreed to free the humans that wanted to be freed.
